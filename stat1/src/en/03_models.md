# Models and training

## Multilingual Pose Encoder

The Multilingual Pose Encoder (MPE, Fig. 3) takes a clip of $T = 32$ frames with 85 keypoints per frame and outputs a $D$-dimensional clip embedding. Normalisation is part of the model and is exported with it. After the $y$ coordinates are scaled by the frame aspect ratio, each keypoint $\mathbf{p}$ is described in a body frame shared by all points,
$$\mathbf{x}^{body} = \frac{1}{w}\,\mathbf{R}_{\theta}\,(\mathbf{p} - \mathbf{c}),\qquad(1)$$
where $\mathbf{c}$ is the midpoint of the shoulders, $w$ the shoulder width and $\mathbf{R}_{\theta}$ rotates the shoulder line to the horizontal, with $\mathbf{c}$, $w$ and $\theta$ averaged over the clip with the pose mask as weights, and in a local frame that describes the shape of each hand and of the face independently of its position; for a hand,
$$\mathbf{x}^{loc} = \frac{\mathbf{p} - \mathbf{p}_{0}}{\lVert \mathbf{p}_{9} - \mathbf{p}_{0} \rVert},\qquad(2)$$
where $\mathbf{p}_0$ is the wrist and $\mathbf{p}_9$ the base of the middle finger; for the face the origin is the nose tip, the scale the distance between the iris centres and the rotation aligns the irises. Scales are bounded below by $10^{-3}$ and coordinates clipped to $[-10, 10]$. Each point is thus represented by six features $(x^{body}, y^{body}, x^{loc}, y^{loc}, m, c)$, with $m$ the validity mask and $c$ the detector confidence (pose visibility, or the presence flag of the part).

A subset of 27 points forms a graph: the nose, the two iris centres, the shoulders and elbows, and ten points of each hand (wrist, base and tip of the thumb, index, middle and little fingers, and the tip of the ring finger), with body edges along the arms, hand edges along the kinematic chain and three long-range edges that connect the two wrists and each index-finger tip with the nose. Following ST-GCN [@2018_yan_stgcn], the neighbourhood of each node is split into the node itself, neighbours closer to the nose and neighbours farther from it, giving normalised adjacency matrices $\widehat{\mathbf{A}}_{k} = \mathbf{\Lambda}_{k}^{-1/2}\mathbf{A}_{k}\mathbf{\Lambda}_{k}^{-1/2}$, $k = 1, 2, 3$, and a graph convolution block
$$\mathbf{H}' = \mathrm{ReLU}\Big(\mathrm{BN}\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{3}\widehat{\mathbf{A}}_{k}\,\mathbf{H}\,\mathbf{W}_{k}\Big) + \mathrm{Res}(\mathbf{H})\Big),\qquad(3)$$
where $\mathrm{Res}$ is the identity or a $1 \times 1$ projection. Three blocks with 64, 128 and $D$ channels operate on each frame; a joint stream and a bone stream (differences between each node and its parent in a tree rooted at the nose) are summed node by node.

The 27 node embeddings and the 58 points outside the graph are grouped into four *body-part tokens* per frame, left hand, right hand, body and face, each the sum of the mask-weighted mean of its graph-node embeddings, a two-layer multilayer perceptron (MLP) over the six features of its remaining points, and a learned part embedding; an absent part is masked. The four tokens are fused by attention with a learned query over the present parts, a learned positional embedding is added, and the sequence passes through a depthwise temporal convolution (kernel 5) and $N$ Conformer layers [@2020_gulati_conformer] (half-step feed-forward modules with expansion 4, four-head self-attention with a frame mask, a convolution module with kernel 15, dropout 0.1). Attention pooling over time gives the clip embedding
$$\alpha_{t} = \operatorname{softmax}_{t}\big(\mathbf{w}^{\top}\tanh(\mathbf{W}\mathbf{h}_{t})\big),\qquad \mathbf{z} = \sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_{t}\,\mathbf{h}_{t}.\qquad(4)$$
A linear classifier is attached to $\mathbf{z}$ for each dataset; when several datasets are trained together, each example contributes a cross-entropy loss only through the head of its own dataset. Two auxiliary heads are used during training only, a normalised 256-dimensional embedding trained with cosine logits against learned class prototypes (scale 16) and one classifier per part token, so that the training loss is
$$\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{CE} + \lambda_{e}\,\mathcal{L}_{emb} + \beta\sum_{j=1}^{4}\mathcal{L}_{part,j},\qquad(5)$$
with $\lambda_e = 0.5$ and $\beta = 0.1$.

The base configuration MPE-A0 uses $D = 192$ and $N = 3$: 4.41 M parameters in training and 3.38 M at inference, 0.76 GFLOPs per 32-frame window (counted with `torch.utils.flop_counter`, a multiply–add as two operations). The main configuration MPE-A1 adds an exponential moving average (EMA) of the weights for validation and testing [@1992_polyak_averaging], with decay 0.999 for budgets of at least 3,000 steps and 0.99 otherwise; MPE-A2 replaces the graph convolution blocks with a per-part MLP (3.14 M parameters, 0.196 GFLOPs). A larger variant with $D = 256$ and $N = 6$ (10.27 M parameters, 1.33 GFLOPs) is used in the capacity study.

## Baselines

All baselines receive the same keypoint files and folds. **ST-GCN** [@2018_yan_stgcn] and **SL-GCN** [@2021_jiang_sam_slr] are run from the code released by their authors (repository yysijie/st-gcn, commit 221c0e15; the joint stream of the SAM-SLR code, commit de6c53a6) on our 27 keypoints, both inside our training loop with the shared recipe below and a 32-frame window (3.34 M and 5.07 M parameters; 1.98 and 2.19 GFLOPs), and with their authors' training configurations (for SL-GCN: SGD, 250 epochs, random 100-frame windows in training and 150 frames at test time, DropGraph; for ST-GCN: the configuration of its repository, which tests on a 300-frame buffer). Our earlier re-implementations of both networks on the 27-node graph and the input features of MPE are reported for comparison but are not used as baselines, for the reason given in Section 6.1. The **keypoint Transformer** is a pre-norm Transformer encoder [@2017_vaswani_attention] with a classification token over 67 points per frame (25 upper-body pose points and both hands), $D = 256$, four layers and a 64-frame window (3.53 M parameters, 0.43 GFLOPs), trained with the shared recipe and with a recipe developed for it earlier (selected on validation data); the better recipe is reported. Three simple baselines, a two-layer Transformer, a bidirectional LSTM with attention pooling and an MLP, use the shared recipe without weight averaging. **MViTv2-S** [@2022_li_mvitv2], the video model released with Slovo, is evaluated only on the official Slovo split as a reference.

## Shared training recipe

All keypoint models are trained with AdamW (learning rate $10^{-3}$, weight decay 0.05), linear warm-up over 5% of the steps and cosine decay to zero, label smoothing 0.1, batch size 128 and bfloat16 mixed precision; the prototype-embedding loss is part of the shared recipe for all models, whereas part heads exist only in MPE. Budgets are defined in optimiser steps and are identical for all models compared on a dataset, $\lceil 100\,N_{train}/128 \rceil$ with $N_{train}$ the largest training set among the folds (14,488 steps on Slovo, 2,154 on Bukva, about 100 epochs). During training a 32-frame window is sampled with a random temporal rate between ×0.5 and ×2; frames are dropped with probability 0.1 and duplicated with probability 0.3, sign boundaries are shifted by up to five frames, a hand is removed in 20–25% of the frames, and the keypoints are rotated (±15°), scaled (0.9–1.1), shifted (±0.05 of the frame width) and, for the hands, jittered with Gaussian noise ($\sigma$ = 0.002). At test time 32 frames are sampled uniformly over the clip. The checkpoint with the best validation top-1 accuracy is tested once; seeds 0, 1 and 2 control initialisation, batch order and augmentation. All models were trained on one NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5080 (16 GB) with PyTorch 2.14.1; one MPE-A1 run takes about 4 min on Slovo. GPU training is not bit-exact deterministic, and re-running a configuration changes the result by about as much as changing the seed.

## Transfer to UzSL and budgets fixed in advance

Three pretraining sources are compared, Slovo, Bukva, and both together with separate heads; the encoder is pretrained on all recordings of the source with 5% of the source signers held out for checkpoint selection. Three schemes are compared on the target: training from scratch, full fine-tuning (the encoder is frozen for the first 20% of the steps and then unfrozen layer by layer every 7% of the steps, with a backbone learning rate of 0.1 times the head rate) and a linear probe with a frozen encoder. With two or three participants, a validation set drawn from the training participants measures the wrong thing (Section 6.6), so the training budgets were fixed before any pilot or corpus run from a simulation on Bukva under SD-5 with four recordings per class: the budget is the median, over 15 runs, of the step with the best validation accuracy, 1,466.2 epochs for training from scratch and 209.5 epochs for fine-tuning and the linear probe. On the target, the number of steps is $\lceil \text{epochs} \times N_{train}/128 \rceil$, the model of the last step is evaluated, no target data are used for model selection, and the budgets were committed to version control before the first run. For short runs the EMA decay is warmed up as $\min(0.99, (1+t)/(10+t))$ at step $t$. On the pilot, each condition is run with three seeds on both folds (test participant 002 or 003), pretraining being repeated for each seed; on the corpus, the same budgets give 5,389 steps for the pretrained models with the 682-class training set, 9,482 with all recordings, and 37,720 and 66,368 steps for training from scratch. The released SL-GCN was transferred with exactly the same folds, recipe and budgets after its own pretraining on Slovo and Bukva.
